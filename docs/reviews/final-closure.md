# Final closure: the originals, AS1 and AS2 (2026-11-21)

The owner supplied the three works the remaining holds waited for — Walton, *A Pragmatic Theory of Fallacy* (1995, PDF and EPUB), Walton, *Goal-Based Reasoning for Argumentation* (2015, EPUB) and Walton, "The Sunk Costs Fallacy or Argument from Waste", *Argumentation* 16 (2002, PDF) — and asked whether the library should have both a verified AS1 and a full AS2 model. It should, and now has both. This review records what the originals settle, what was added and changed, how it was verified and validated, and what is not claimed.

The files were treated as untrusted input: each was unpacked into its own directory and read as text, with the PDF pages of Atkinson and Bench-Capon (2007) rendered as images where the text extraction loses symbols (≠ in the formal critical questions, the labels of Figs. 1-8). Only page references and short phrases are quoted here.

**Reviewer.** Claude, the implementer, who also wrote every change below. The decision D14 asks that someone who did not write a change review it; that is not met for these eight records, and every stamp says so. The owner's independent research, announced for when the gaps are closed, is the second check.

## 2. AS1 and AS2: why both

AS1 (p. 858) is the scheme a person states in a dialogue: circumstances, action, new circumstances, goal, value, with seventeen critical questions (CQ17 added on p. 859). AS2 (p. 861) restates it on an action-based alternating transition system (AATS) extended with values (pp. 860-861), where every critical question has a precise definition (p. 862). The first is usable without a model and is what the compendium-style library needs; the second is what lets the questions be answered rather than only asked. They are separate records because they are separate claims: an AS1 argument does not say which joint action, which agents or which transition it relies on.

Five records are added, filed under a new origin, `publication` (a scheme taken from a publication other than the compendium, not in Carneades' own file; listed in `publication_only`, citing its pages, with the number of questions it prints):

* `practical_reasoning_as1` — AS1. CQ1-CQ4 are premises, CQ12-CQ16 defaults, CQ10 support, CQ8, CQ9 and CQ11 rebuttals by the two negative versions, CQ5-CQ7 alternatives (below), CQ17 the premise of CQ2, under which the paper says the source of AS1 subsumed it (p. 859).
* `practical_reasoning_as1_demotion` and `practical_reasoning_as1_preclusion` — the two negative versions p. 859 uses to pose CQ8/CQ9 and CQ11. The paper says they are "subject to the same set of critical questions"; their sorting, mirrored for side effects (a further demotion adds to the reason to refrain, a promotion rebuts it), is this project's. The demotion version states the features it avoids with `would_obtain(G,S)`, not `would_be_realized(G,S)`: with the latter, `practical_reasoning2` would take a feature to be avoided for the agent's goal and argue for the action (found by the reference case, which failed until this was changed).
* `practical_reasoning_as2` and `practical_reasoning_as2_demotion` — AS2 and its negative version over the AATS as facts: current state, states, joint actions and their parts, preconditions, transitions, the propositions a transition changes, the propositions true somewhere, the agent's values, and promotion and demotion through a goal. CQ17 is an exception for the positive version and the possibility of the joint action for the negative one (below).

Two representation choices are declared in the ledger. The conclusion is the agent's part of the joint action (`should_perform(I,Al)`; AS2 says "participate in joint action jn where jn_i = α_i"), so that arguments about different joint actions with the same part meet in one issue. And each promotion or demotion is attached to the goal that realizes it: the paper's δ is a function of two states and a value, but Fig. 6 labels one transition both +P and −P and footnote 5 (p. 868) counts "two harmful goals" as a severer demotion.

## 3. Alternatives and the comparison contract

CQ5-CQ7 and, on the AATS, CQ11 are answered by arguments for other actions; CQ8 and CQ9 by arguments against this one. The paper compares them by an audience's value order (pp. 859, 862). The engine cannot: it gives each applicable argument of these schemes weight 1, so a reason to refrain blocks a reason to act and arguments for different actions are all accepted. The ledger's `alternative` kind used to refuse completion for that reason. It is now accepted when the row names an executable comparison contract — the only one is `value-based-choice` (`docs/contracts.md`, contract 8), implemented by `tools/aats.py` — and the alternative names the record's own conclusion; the suite checks both, and the approval fingerprint covers the contract's name.

The rule: for each of the agent's actions, take the joint actions it may face (those no assurance of the agent excludes); for each value of the audience, score the least over them of the goals promoting the value minus those demoting it; choose the actions whose scores, in the audience's order, are greatest. Contract 8 lists what in the paper each part of the rule reads, and what it does not claim (it is not a general VAF evaluator, and it looks one step ahead, as the paper does).

## 4. CQ17

CQ17 (p. 862) holds when another joint action with the same part for the agent leads elsewhere. The paper answers it in two ways: the value is promoted whatever the others do (Obj2bi, p. 865; the eastbound train in q7, p. 871), or the agent is assured that they will not choose otherwise (a social law, or their own interests, p. 871). `tools/aats.py` therefore states the exception `others_may_prevent(Q0,J,I,V)` only for a value that some unexcluded deviation does not promote, and drops excluded joint actions from `possible_joint_action`. A reason to refrain is not undercut by CQ17: a possible demotion is a reason (the trains in q5 and q6, p. 871), unless an assurance excludes it. This asymmetry is this project's reading, stated in the ledger.

## 5. Verification and validation

* **The models.** `tools/aats_models/farmer.yml` (Fig. 1, the nine choice points of the reformulated problem, Figs. 2-8 and the text of pp. 866-869) and `tools/aats_models/trains.yml` (Table 1, seven choice points of p. 871). `tools/aats.py` validates each against the AATS definitions: Q non-empty, q0 in Q, pairwise disjoint actions, a joint action has one action of each agent, τ a function into Q that agrees with ρ, every agent has values, every goal changes, every effect's value is a value, states told apart by what holds in them, an assurance cannot remove one of the agent's own actions. `tools/test_aats.py` rejects a mutation of each.
* **The engine builds exactly the adapter's arguments.** `tools/aats.py cases --write` states each of the 17 situations as facts and lists every AS2 argument; the case field `exact_schemes` (new, with its own mutation test) makes the suite fail if Carneades builds one more or one fewer. All 17 pass, in process and through the command's round trip, with the labels the adapter predicts for the engine's own weighing.
* **The paper's choices.** All 30 choices the paper reports, under the preference for progress and for friendship, with and without assurances, and two consistent choices for Fig. 1. They do not depend on the order of chicken and seeds, which the paper leaves open.
* **A second derivation.** `tools/test_aats.py` recomputes every choice from the generated case file alone (facts and argument identifiers) and compares it with the adapter, on the paper's models and on 300 random ones; it rewrites the formal definitions of CQ5-CQ11 and CQ17 as set comprehensions and compares them with the adapter's objections on the same models.
* **The paper's arguments and objections.** Fig. 1 yields exactly Arg1-Arg8; Obj1a, Obj1b, Obj2a, Obj2b (answered by Obj2bi), Obj2d, Obj2f, Obj3a, Obj4a, Obj4b, Obj4c, Obj5a, Obj6a and Obj7a are found with their questions; the formulation questions of p. 864 (the bridge, CQ13; the muzzled dog, CQ12, CQ14 and CQ15; a state q_n with n > 9, CQ2; another initial state, CQ1), Obj1c (CQ16), Obj2c (CQ4) and the CQ3 objection to Arg10 (p. 867) are answered for claimed arguments.
* **The tests can fail.** Six seeded defects in the adapter (max for min in the rule, CQ17 inverted, CQ9 for CQ8, CQ5 inverted, CQ3 removed, assurances ignored) each make `tools/test_aats.py` fail.
* **AS1.** `cases/practical_as1.yml`: the diet example of p. 858, each of CQ12-CQ16 questioned (and CQ16 answered), CQ10's second value, CQ5-CQ7 left to the contract, the two negative versions (Obj5a, Obj2f), and AS2 cases written by hand (Arg8, an unanswered CQ17, Obj5a, an assured joint action).

## 6. Findings about the paper

1. The example says it gives "at least one example of each of the seventeen critical questions" (p. 864) but labels thirteen: CQ5, CQ7, CQ10 and CQ15 are never named. CQ7 and CQ10 occur in the model (Obj4b; Arg5-Arg7); CQ5 and CQ15 are tested on constructed models and claims.
2. Obj4b (p. 865) and the move in q10 (p. 868) are called CQ6, reading "putting a possession on the right bank" as one goal. On the AATS a goal is a proposition, and the objection meets CQ7's definition (same value, another goal); CQ6's holds only for the same proposition. The tests record both.
3. Fig. 5 shows one −P on q11 to q10 and Fig. 7 one −P on q15 to q14, where the text counts two (Arg15, "severely"; "in two ways", p. 868). The models follow the text.
4. Rowing the chicken back from q9 to q0 (Fig. 3) and from q18 to q16 revisit states, but the text counts only the move to the left bank. The models follow the text; counting the revisit changes no choice (tested).
5. The trains' progress "demoted by staying still" (p. 870) has no proposition in Table 1 that changes, so AS2 has no goal for it — the gap the paper closes for the farmer by counting non-movement as repetition (p. 867). It is left out; adding it changes no choice (tested).
6. The paper says the state lists the right bank first and the left second (p. 863), but writes the initial state [BCDS, _] and moves possessions into the second list; the models read the first list as the starting (left) bank.

## 7. Not claimed

The comparison rule is a reading of the paper's narrative, validated against the choices it reports, not an algorithm the paper states. The AS2 records need the model's facts; they are meant to be fed by `tools/aats.py` or another generator of the same facts, and say nothing about how a model is built. `practical_reasoning1`, `practical_reasoning2` and `practical_reasoning_as1` share premises, so in one graph questioning an AS1 default does not remove a conclusion that `practical_reasoning2` still supports; the cases select the record they test. Approval binds the scheme, its templates and its ledger contract; it does not authenticate the reviewer, and the reviewer here is the implementer.
